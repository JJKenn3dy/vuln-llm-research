## Verdict
- **GO on the method.** Clean dual-teacher labels lifted the small model 0.511 -> 0.695 on real
  out-of-domain data, with FAR held at 4% (lower than Opus/GPT-5.5). The paranoia is gone on
  general triage. Achieved on HALF the data of exp4 (236 vs 448) and 1/4 the unique TP (56 vs 204),
  cut short at 29% of the available pool. Quality of labels >> quantity.
- **One blind spot: XSS/escaping.** Fails on BOTH XSS tests (synthetic controlled FAR 90%,
  real mined MCC ~0). Not a method failure; a data-composition + recipe artifact:
  1. training pool had no matched escaping pairs (safe-only-because-escaped), unlike exp4's CyberNative;
  2. my TP x3 oversample biased the model to lean TP on short/ambiguous snippets.

## Fix plan -> exp6
1. **Blend data**: exp5 dual-teacher general set + synthetic escaping pairs (CyberNative, proven in exp4)
   + real mined XSS pairs. Restore the escaping-contrast signal.
2. **Drop/soften TP oversampling.** Instead get real TP by finishing the labeling.
3. **Scale labeling**: only 277/970 (29%) of pool used before credits died. Top up OpenRouter (~$15-20),
   label the rest -> ~600-800 consensus, ~150+ unique TP -> recall should close toward flagships.
   Use GLM-5.2 as primary labeler (10x cheaper, near-equal, less paranoid than GPT-5.5).
4. Keep the stand fixed for honest OOD eval. Re-run on RunPod (~$0.5).

## Teacher head-to-head (why GLM is the workhorse labeler)
- GPT-5.5 slightly more accurate (0.909 vs 0.868) but SYSTEMATICALLY paranoid (all errors = false positives).
- GLM-5.2 balanced errors, escaping 20/20 perfect, FAR 4%, 10x cheaper. Better aligned to a
  false-positive-cutting product. Dual (agree-only) neutralizes both biases.
